## "INTEGRATE (Feel)"—Full 6-Phase Wavelength Breakdown

Teal's Wavelength has its own six phases, with its own medicine and its own poison. Where Yellow did the work, Teal lets the work arrive—which is not the same as easy. Where Yellow was directed, Teal is receptive. Let's walk through how each phase *feels* at Teal.

### 1. RISING

**Rx: Epiphany | OD: Delusion**

At Yellow, Rising was Rebellion—something you *did*. At Teal, Rising is Epiphany—something that *arrives*.

You don't reason your way to the answer. You're walking, and the answer is simply there. Whole. Like a bird landing on a branch. You didn't make it land. It just chose to.

This is the shift from doing to receiving. You're still present with the question (the walk, the body, the field), but you're not working it. You're *available* to it.

**What This Feels Like in Practice:**

You set out on your walk. The first few minutes, your mind is scattered—lists of things to do, snippets of conversations, worries about the future. This is normal. At Yellow, you would *corral* the attention, firmly redirecting it back to the present. At Teal, you just... notice the scattering. You don't fight it.

And then—without forcing it—something lands. Maybe a crow calls and the thing you've been circling for weeks suddenly has a shape. Maybe the rhythm of your footsteps shakes a sentence loose. Maybe you asked a question at the start of the walk and, twenty minutes in, the answer walks up beside you like the dog.

The key word is "lands." You don't *grab* the insight. It finds you.

This is Epiphany. It's what happens when you stop trying to figure it out and let the knowing come. The True Self *wants* to be heard. You just have to stop talking over it.

**Recognizing and Releasing Delusion:**

But sometimes—especially for those of us whose brains love a good pattern—the knowing comes in too hot. Every crow is a message. Every coincidence is a sign. The insight that landed on Tuesday has, by Thursday, become a theory of your whole life that nobody is allowed to question.

Delusion feels like certainty. It has a quality of rush, even when it's "spiritual." There's a subtle grasping in it—a need to make the insight *bigger*, to pin it to everything, to be the one who sees.

When Delusion arises at Teal, it feels like speed. A subtle inflation. You notice it because you've trained the capacity to sense when the personality has grabbed the mic back and is doing a very good impression of the True Self. And you soften. You don't argue with it—you just *set it down* for now.

How? By checking it against the ground. Does this knowing make you more able to do the dishes, or less? Would you still say it out loud to a friend who loves you and isn't impressed by you? Does it hold up tomorrow, after sleep? Real Epiphany survives those questions. Delusion needs you to not ask them.

This is the medicine of Teal's Rising: knowing that arrives whole. Not because you've stopped thinking, but because you've learned to trust something underneath the thinking—and to check it.

**Off the Cushion: Epiphany in Daily Life**

You're in a conversation. At Orange, you'd be actively listening—tracking what they're saying, formulating your response, managing your body language. That's skillful, but it's effortful.

At Teal, you just... listen. And somewhere in the middle of their sentence, you *know* what's actually going on with them—the thing under the thing they're saying. You didn't deduce it. It arrived. And you can check it: "Is it that you're scared of letting him down?" Sometimes you're right. Sometimes you're not, and you let it go without needing to have been.

Or you're stuck on a decision. At Yellow, you'd map it—pros, cons, second-order effects, leverage points. At Teal, you take it on the walk and ask. And either an answer comes, or it doesn't, and you wait.

On the days Rising goes well at Teal, knowing becomes play. It's light, curious, responsive. You're not grimly concentrating. You're delighted by what's arriving, moment by moment. And that delight is what keeps the line open—not willpower, but genuine interest in what's alive right now.

### 2. PEAKING

**Rx: Gnosis | OD: Psychosis**

At Teal, Gnosis is not something you achieve. It's something you *recognize*.

The question you carried onto the walk stops being a question. Not because you solved it, but because you *know*—the way you know your own name. What this is. What to do. Why you're here. And the knowing didn't come from the part of you that argues.

This is where bliss arises naturally. But at Teal, you don't grasp at it. You let it come. You let it go. Like a wave passing through.

**The Phenomenology of Gnosis:**

You're 30 minutes into your walk. The scattered thoughts have quieted. The commentary has stopped. And suddenly—though there's no "suddenly," it happened so gradually you didn't notice—you're just... here. And something is speaking.

Not in words, usually. More like the answer was already in the room and you finally turned around.

Your chest is warm. Your jaw has let go. Your feet keep walking without you supervising them. The trees aren't scenery anymore—they're company, the way a good friend is company. And the knowing sits in you lower than your thoughts do, somewhere around the sternum, and it doesn't hurry.

This is Gnosis at Teal. Not because you've narrowed your attention to a point, but because a knowing has arrived whole—what to do, what this is, why you're here—and it didn't come from the part of you that argues.

**When Bliss Arises—and How Not to Grasp It:**

Sometimes, with the knowing, bliss arises. It can be subtle—a gentle warmth spreading through your chest, a softening in your face, a felt sense of *rightness*. Or it can be intense—waves of euphoria, tears of gratitude, a body-wide sensation of love pouring through you.

At Yellow, you'd be tempted to analyze it: "What caused this? How do I replicate it? This must mean I'm doing it right."

At Teal, you just... let it be. You don't grab for it. You don't try to make it stay. You don't even label it as "bliss." You just feel it fully, completely, without adding story.

This is the medicine of Peaking at Teal: you can receive profound guidance without *inflating* around it. The knowing is real. The bliss is real. And both are weather.

**Psychosis at Teal: Losing the Thread**

Psychosis is the strong word on purpose. At its mild end, it's the moment the inner voice stops being one voice among many and becomes the only one you'll listen to. At its far end, it's the thread between inner guidance and shared reality actually snapping—and people who walk this path do, sometimes, lose it. This is not a metaphor. If you have a history of mania or psychosis, please work this stage alongside a therapist who knows you.

It looks like this: you're in the knowing. Bliss is present. And then a thought arises: "This is it. I've been *chosen*. Everyone else is asleep and I'm the one who sees..."

And immediately, something shifts. The knowing, which was quiet and certain, gets loud and urgent. There's "you" with a *message* now. The humility drains out of it.

The antidote is the same test the Mood chapter gave you: does the guidance ever tell you to do something tedious? Real Gnosis sends you home to call your mother and fix the gutter. It's oddly practical. Psychosis sends you to tell everyone what you've realized. And the other test: can it survive being said out loud to someone who isn't impressed by you? If it can't—if the knowing shrinks the moment another person is in the room—set it down. Eat something. Sleep. See if it's still true in the morning.

Over time, you get faster at feeling the difference. Real knowing is calm and done after one sentence. The counterfeit keeps talking.

**The Teaching of Impermanence:**

Peaking at Teal teaches you viscerally what Buddhism has been saying all along: *anicca*—everything changes. Even the clearest knowing you've ever had will fade back into an ordinary Tuesday. It arises, it peaks, it passes. Always.

This isn't a problem. This isn't failure. This is just how reality works.

And when you truly understand this—not intellectually, but *experientially*—you stop chasing the state. You stop measuring your practice by whether the voice showed up today.

Peaking at Teal is *light*. You're certain, but not rigid. Guided, but not inflated. Blissful, but not attached.

**Off the Cushion: Knowing Without Inflating**

You're in a moment of genuine clarity—about a relationship, a decision, your work, your life. The old pattern would be to think, "Finally. Now I know. Now I'll never be confused again."

At Teal, you don't think that. You just... act on it. Fully. Simply. Knowing that this clarity is already fading even as you're in it, and that you'll have to ask again next week. And somehow, that knowledge makes it *more* trustworthy, not less.

You're not building a cathedral on one afternoon's insight. You're doing the one next thing it pointed to. And when the clarity ends, you let it end, and turn toward the next question with the same quality of listening.

This is what it means to peak without losing the thread. To receive the fullness of guidance without needing to be special for it.

### 3. WITHDRAWAL

**Rx: Receptivity | OD: Paranoia**

At Yellow, Withdrawal was Organize—you noticed the drift and put things back in order. At Teal, Withdrawal is the knowing going quiet. And the medicine is not to chase it. It's to stay *open*.

The voice recedes. You don't scold yourself or try to summon it back. You just… keep listening. Not because you expect an answer, but because listening is the whole practice, silence included.

**The Natural Fade:**

You've been walking for 40 minutes. You were in a beautiful flow—guided, present, certain. And then... you're not. A thought pulls you in. A planning spiral starts. Or you just space out—daydreaming, drifting, gone. And the knowing you had ten minutes ago feels like something you read about once.

Earlier in your practice, this would trigger self-judgment: "I lost it. I failed. I need to concentrate harder."

At Teal, it's just: "Oh. It's gone quiet." No drama. No story. Just a neutral observation, and an open hand.

And here's the magic: the moment you notice the silence *without panicking about it*, you're already receptive again. The noticing *itself* is openness. You don't need to do anything else.

This is Receptivity. The line is still there. It's just resting.

**The Somatic Feel of Receptivity:**

Receptivity at Teal has a particular body sensation. It's like a gentle unclenching—not a lurch or a jolt, but a soft opening of the hands and the chest.

Maybe your shoulders drop. Maybe your breath deepens. Maybe your eyes soften. There's a felt sense of "okay, I'm here, whenever you are." Like leaving a window open on a warm night—nothing's coming through right now, but the room is ready.

Pay attention to this feeling. Because over time, you can start to recognize when you're clenching around the silence *before* you've fully closed. You'll feel the pre-clench—a slight bracing, a subtle "why isn't it working"—and you'll open your hand again before it's a fist.

**Paranoia: Reading Signs in the Silence:**

Paranoia at Teal happens when the pattern-reading mind keeps reading after the signal has gone. The knowing went quiet, and instead of letting it rest, you start interpreting the quiet. "It left because I did something wrong." "The crow flew *left* today—that's a warning." "My partner doesn't understand this, so maybe my partner is the obstacle."

Here's the difference:

**Receptivity:** You notice the silence. You see that the guidance isn't coming right now. You're fully aware that you're not in the knowing. And you're okay with that—not as an excuse to check out, but as a recognition that this is what's arising right now. From that place, you keep walking, keep listening, keep your life ordinary. You're not forcing. But you're also not reading the tea leaves.

**Paranoia:** You notice the silence. And you decide it *means* something. Every ambiguous sign becomes evidence. The silence is a punishment, a test, a message, a threat. You're still walking, but you're not listening anymore—you're decoding.

Receptivity is open. Paranoia is clenched. You feel it in the body: ease, even when nothing is coming, versus a tight, watchful feeling like you're being followed by your own practice.

**How to Recognize Paranoia:**

Ask yourself: Am I *with* the silence, or am I *interrogating* it?

If you're *with* it, you're open. You're walking. You're noticing the wind and the light and the dog, and you're not assigning any of it a meaning. You're present for the quiet. That's receptivity.

If you're interrogating it, you've forgotten you're practicing. You're fully identified with the story the mind is telling about why the voice left and who's to blame. Nobody's listening for the deeper voice. That's the poison.

When you notice Paranoia, you gently come back to the ground. Not with force. Just with a soft return to the feet, the breath, the actual tree in front of you—which is just a tree.

And you forgive yourself. Paranoia is an occupational hazard of a mind that's learned to read the field. The practice is noticing it and opening your hand again. And again.

**Off the Cushion: Staying Open When It Goes Quiet:**

You're in a relationship and the warmth has faded. You're going through the motions—saying "I love you" out of habit, not presence. Having conversations on autopilot. You're not *fighting*, but you're not really *connecting* either.

Paranoia reads the fade as a verdict. "They're pulling away. They've met someone. They've finally seen the real me." You start watching for evidence, and a mind that watches for evidence always finds some.

At Teal, this is just... withdrawal. A natural phase. Not a crisis. Not a sign. Just the tide going out, the way tides do.

So you stay open. You initiate a real conversation. You make eye contact. You touch with intention. You ask a question you're genuinely curious about—instead of the question you already think you know the answer to. Small openings. Gentle availability.

This is Teal's wisdom applied to life: everything goes quiet sometimes. Guidance recedes. Attention scatters. Connection fades. That's not a message. That's just the rhythm. The work is noticing when the silence has started to sound like a threat and choosing, instead, to leave the window open.

### 4. DIMINISHING

**Rx: Absorption | OD: Horror**

At Teal, Diminishing isn't about setting anything up, the way Establish was at Yellow. It's going *under*. The knowing that arrived in Rising and spoke in Peaking stops being something you hear and starts being something you're *soaked in*. You feel it in the body more than the mind. That's Absorption.

Maybe the walk goes wordless today. No insight, no voice, just a heaviness in the legs and a sense of something settling into the bones. You're not planning it—you're *sinking* into it.

**Taking It In:**

You're 40 minutes into your walk. The clarity has passed. The silence has come and gone. And now there's just... depth. The practice feels slow. Thick. Like you're walking through water, and the water is the thing you learned last week, and it's getting into your skin.

At Yellow, you'd push through. You'd double down on the technique. "Focus harder. Concentrate more."

At Teal, you let it take you. You ask: What is this soaking into? And you wait for the body's answer.

Maybe the answer is: the thing you realized about your father on Tuesday is now in your shoulders, and they're finally dropping.

Maybe it's: the "yes" you felt about the move is traveling from your head to your gut, where it'll actually be able to carry you.

This is the art of Teal's Diminishing—trusting that what you were shown is still working on you after you've stopped being able to see it.

**The Difference Between Absorption and Horror:**

But here's the tricky part: going under is also where the descent can turn.

Absorption feels like weight that's *holding* you. It's heavy but it's kind. There's no urgency—just depth. "This is sinking in."

Horror feels like weight that's *crushing* you. It's driven by dread—you saw something true in Peaking (about yourself, about how little you control, about how much you've been running from) and now it's too much. "I can't unsee this. I shouldn't have looked."

Here's a test: when the heaviness comes, pause. Take three breaths. And ask: Is this holding me, or is it holding me *down*?

If it's holding you—if there's a slow, warm, gravity-like quality to it—that's absorption. Stay. Let it steep. Walk slower.

If it's holding you down—if there's a cold, racing, bottomless quality, if the thing you saw has turned into a thing that's *coming for you*—that's horror. Don't tough it out alone. Get your feet on the ground, get a human in the room, and turn the lights on.

**Horror: When the Vision Turns:**

Horror at Teal is the dark night. The insight that opened you in Rising and Peaking has gone too far down without enough ground under it. What was "I am more than my personality" becomes "my personality is a lie and there's nothing underneath." What was "I chose this life" becomes "I chose *this*? Why?" What was *anicca*—everything changes—becomes a terror of dissolving with no floor.

It's the practitioner who had a real opening and then couldn't sleep for a week. Who can't stop seeing the bones under every face. Who's suddenly afraid of their own kitchen at night.

The irony is that horror often masquerades as depth. "I'm just in the dark night of the soul. This is the real work." And sometimes it is. But the dark night has a ground floor, and if you've fallen through it, the holy thing to do is call someone. Absorption is heaviness getting into your bones. Horror is the signal that it's getting in faster than the bones can take it.

Because here's what the seasoned practitioners know: the heaviness usually *is* the thing working. One day, for no apparent reason, it lifts, and you find you're carrying what you saw instead of being carried off by it. It was composting something.

**When to Stay, When to Get Help:**

So how do you know?

**Stay if:**
- The weight has a quality of ripening. You can feel something working beneath the surface, even if it's not pleasant.
- You're heavy, but not frightened. Heaviness is often just a knowing settling in. See if you can stay with it a little longer.
- You can still eat, sleep, and laugh at the dog.

**Get help if:**
- The practice is causing harm. If it's triggering trauma, destabilizing your nervous system, or making you less functional, stop. Find support. Choose something gentler.
- The dread has lasted for days and there's no floor. Not just "this is heavy," but "I can't find the ground." Sometimes what you saw needs a second person to hold it with you.
- You've started to feel that reality itself is unsafe. That's not a stretch to walk alone. Trust that.

Absorption is being steeped. Horror is being drowned. Feel the difference.

**Off the Cushion: Letting It Sink In:**

You've had a real realization—about a job, a relationship, a city. You saw it clearly on the walk: this isn't alive anymore. And now the seeing has faded, but the weight hasn't. You're moving slower. You cry at commercials. You're not depressed, exactly. You're *steeping*.

At Teal, you let it. You don't rush to act on it. You don't rush to talk yourself out of it. You let it get into your gut, where decisions actually get made.

There's no formula. But at Teal, you trust your felt sense—and you trust your people. You let the body tell you when it's done absorbing. And when the knowing has gone all the way down—when it's not an idea anymore but a weight you can stand on—you act.

Sometimes that means staying, now that you've actually felt what it costs. Sometimes it means leaving.

This is the wisdom of Diminishing: letting what you saw get all the way into you. And knowing the difference between going deep and going under.

### 5. BOTTOMING OUT

**Rx: Metabolism | OD: Despair**

At Teal, Metabolism is not resignation. It's digestion. Something got taken in on the walks that worked—an image, a phrase, a yes you felt in your gut—and now the body is breaking it down into something it can actually use. That takes time, and from the outside it looks like nothing. You walk for 45 minutes and nothing comes. You open the journal and the page stays blank. Your legs are heavy. You're not sad, exactly. You're *full*, the way you are after a big meal, and the only thing to do is let it work.

You've stopped trying. Not because you've given up, but because you've realized that there's nowhere to get to right now. You're already here. And here is enough.

This is the wisdom of "just sitting" in Zen. Shikantaza. You're not walking *to* achieve something. You're walking because walking is what's happening right now, and the rest is happening in the dark where you can't watch it.

Despair at Teal is when Metabolism tips into nihilism. When "nowhere to get to" becomes "nothing matters." When digestion becomes collapse. When the blank page stops being a resting stomach and starts being proof that the voice was never real.

The difference is aliveness. Metabolism is alive—slow, but alive. Despair is numb. Metabolism includes sadness, but it's not crushed by it. Despair can't feel anything at all, and it's quietly certain it never will again.

If you're in Despair, reach out. Get support. This is not a phase to push through alone.

### 6. RESTORATION

**Rx: Pattern-Seeking | OD: Belief Salience**

At Teal, Restoration is spontaneous renewal. You didn't plan it. It just happened. The energy returns. The walk feels fresh again. And you start to notice the *shape* of what you've just been through.

This is the gift of trusting the cycle. You didn't have to force Restoration. It came on its own, because it always does.

Pattern-Seeking is the medicine: you start noticing the shape of what just happened to you—what the dryness was composting, what the walks were quietly teaching, why the voice went quiet when it did. You look back over the journal and the entries line up. "Oh. *That's* what that was." You're not inventing a story. You're recognizing one that was already there.

Belief Salience is the poison: you decide the shape is The Truth and stop checking it against anything. The pattern you spotted on Tuesday is, by Friday, a doctrine. It explains your childhood, your ex, the stock market. Every new experience gets folded into it instead of being allowed to be new. It's the craving for the "special" story—preference disguised as revelation.

The difference is whether you'd still let a friend who loves you ask, "Are you sure?"

The medicine is equanimity. You let the good states come. You let them go. You let the patterns show themselves and you hold them loosely. You trust the cycle to come back around—without pretending the low was pleasant, and without needing your story of the low to be the final word.

Pattern-Seeking is curious. Belief Salience is certain. Stay curious.
