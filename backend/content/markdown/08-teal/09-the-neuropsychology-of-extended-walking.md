## The Neuropsychology of Extended Walking

Why does 45 minutes of walking produce different effects than 45 minutes on a cushion?

**1. Bilateral Stimulation**

Walking activates both hemispheres of the brain in rhythm. Left foot, right foot, left foot, right foot. That left-right rhythm helps the brain digest feelings and stitch scattered experiences back together. It's why EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing) uses bilateral stimulation to treat trauma.

When you walk mindfully, you're giving your brain the conditions it needs to integrate, without forcing it.

**2. Diffuse Attention**

In sitting meditation, attention is often focused (on the breath, on a mantra). In walking, attention naturally diffuses. You're aware of the feet, the breath, the sounds, the sights, the sensations. Everything at once.

This trains panoramic awareness—the capacity to hold many things at once without clutching any of them. And a wide, soft attention is exactly the state in which the True Self's quiet voice can get through.

**3. Reduced Self-Referential Thinking**

Studies using fMRI show that extended walking in nature reduces activity in the Default Mode Network—the part of the brain associated with self-referential thought, rumination, and the sense of "me."

Somewhere past the half-hour mark, the DMN quiets. And when the DMN is quiet, the sense of separate self softens. You're still here, still aware, but the story of "me and my problems" fades into background.
