## The Practice Decoded: What Dog Walkin' Shamanism Actually Is

This practice has an unusual name for a reason. It's not "walking meditation" or "nature contemplation." It's **Dog Walkin' Shamanism**—and that matters.

Here's what each word means:

**Dog Walkin'**: This is casual. Relaxed. Not trying. You're not on a Vision Quest. You're just walking the dog. The ordinariness is the point. You're training yourself to find the sacred in the mundane, not by adding special significance, but by removing the filter that says "this moment isn't special enough."

The "dog" part is literal if you have one, metaphorical if you don't. Dogs are natural meditation teachers. They're fully present—completely absorbed in the smell of that tree, the sight of that squirrel, the feeling of wind on their face. They don't narrate their experience. They don't worry about yesterday or plan for tomorrow. They're *here*.

When you walk with a dog, you're walking with a Zen master who happens to have four legs. They pull you out of your head just by being themselves. You watch them be fully alive in this moment, and something in you remembers: *Oh right. This is how to be.*

If you don't have a dog, the practice still works. You're just borrowing their consciousness as a template. You're asking: What would it be like to walk through the world with that quality of presence? Curious. Embodied. Undefended. Delighted by simple things.

**Shamanism**: This is the practice of moving between worlds. The shaman walks between the human realm and the spirit realm, bringing back medicine. At Teal, you're walking between the personality (the "you" with a to-do list) and the True Self (the you that chose this life and knows why). You're learning to move fluidly between them—and to carry something back each time.

Shamanism, across cultures, shares a core technique: the *journey*. The practitioner enters a liminal state—through drumming, dancing, plant medicine, or trance—and travels to non-ordinary reality. There, they meet guides, ancestors, power animals. They receive teachings. They retrieve lost soul parts. And then they return, bringing the medicine back to serve their community.

Dog Walkin' Shamanism uses the same shape, but the tool is simpler: walking. No ayahuasca. No peyote. Just your body, the earth, and 45 minutes of paying attention.

The journey is from "I am this personality getting through the day" to "I am also the one who chose this day, and that one has something to say." And back again. Over and over. Until the two can talk. Until you can hold both at once.

When you walk for 45 minutes with full presence, something shifts. Your own deeper knowing gets louder than the chatter. The land stops being scenery and starts feeling like a conversation partner—the crow, the shift in the wind, the particular tree you keep ending up under—and the part of you that answers is older than your personality.

This is not mysticism. It's direct experience. And 45 minutes is the threshold where it starts to happen naturally.

**Why 45 Minutes?**

There's neurological research supporting this threshold. The Default Mode Network (DMN)—the part of your brain that constructs the sense of "self"—takes a good half hour or more of sustained, non-demanding activity to quiet down.

For the first 15 minutes of a walk, you're usually still in your head. Planning. Ruminating. Narrating. The DMN is humming along, maintaining the story of "you."

Around 25 minutes, the mental chatter starts to thin. You're still thinking, but the thoughts are less sticky. They arise and pass more easily.

By 35 minutes or so, if you've stayed present, something shifts. The brain's self-narrator goes quiet. The personality loosens its grip. You're still here, still you, but the small self is no longer hogging the mic—and in that quiet, the True Self gets a word in.

45 minutes gives you time to cross that threshold and stay there a while. To actually *listen* to the True Self long enough that your nervous system learns this is a real line, not a one-off.

This is why Dog Walkin' Shamanism asks for the full 45 minutes, no headphones. You need the duration to cross over. To complete the journey.
